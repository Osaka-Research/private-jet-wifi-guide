How to Fix Jet Wi-Fi

"No internet" in a cabin almost never means one broken thing. It means one of three separate boxes — the modem, the access point, or the sky — stopped talking. Find which one before you touch anything.

The distinction that matters: cabin Wi-Fi is a local wireless LAN broadcast by an access point in the cabin management system (CMS). It has nothing to do with connectivity by itself — that comes from a separate modem (ATG or SATCOM) that hands the AP its uplink. You can have a perfect, strong SSID signal with zero data behind it, and the fix for each failure is different.

Know your install

Before troubleshooting, confirm which system is on the tail — it's usually placarded near the CMS panel or in the aircraft flight manual supplement (AFMS):

  • Gogo AVANCE L5 / LX — air-to-ground (ATG), CONUS-only, hands off between ground towers as you fly. Drops near borders and over open water.
  • Honeywell JetWave (Inmarsat GX Aviation) — Ka-band SATCOM, global coverage, needs a clear antenna view of the satellite through the tail or belly radome.
  • Viasat Ka-band — regional beams; performance falls off hard near a beam's edge and in heavy precipitation (rain fade).
  • Starlink Aviation — LEO Ku/Ka, newest install base, most squawks so far trace to terminal firmware, not the link itself.

Quick-fix checklist

  1. 01Power-cycle the cabin AP, not the modem.Most "no Wi-Fi" calls are a hung access point. Reset it first — usually a recessed button on the CMS rack or a breaker labeled CAB WLAN.
  2. 02Read the modem's link light.Green/steady = synced to network. Amber blinking = acquiring. Red = no sync — this is a connectivity fault, not a Wi-Fi fault, and rebooting the AP won't fix it.
  3. 03Check the sky, not the router.SATCOM loses lock in a steep bank away from the satellite, under heavy icing, or with a de-ice fluid film over the radome. ATG loses signal past the coastline or over mountainous terrain shadowing the nearest tower.
  4. 04Rejoin the correct SSID.Cabin devices sometimes latch onto the crew/ops network by habit, which is bandwidth-capped or firewalled separately from the passenger SSID.
  5. 05Check the account, not the aircraft.Gogo and Satcom Direct plans lapse or throttle on data caps. A fully healthy system with an expired plan looks identical, from the cabin, to a broken one.
  6. 06Pull and reset the CMS breaker once.One cycle, then stop. Repeated breaker resets on a persistent fault mask the real failure from maintenance and can trip a latching fault code.
  7. 07Log the LRU and light color, then hand off.Antenna, radome, and modem-bay work is line-maintenance only. Note the part number off the unit's dataplate and the exact fault light state for the technician — it cuts diagnosis time in half.

Glossary

  • ATG — air-to-ground: connectivity via terrestrial cell towers instead of satellite, CONUS coverage only.
  • SATCOM — satellite communications: Ka- or Ku-band link to a geostationary or LEO satellite constellation.
  • MCU — modem control unit: the LRU that manages the SATCOM or ATG radio link.
  • CMS — cabin management system: controls lighting, entertainment, and the cabin Wi-Fi access point.
  • LRU — line-replaceable unit: any box swappable on the ramp without a shop visit.
  • BUC — block up-converter: the transmit-side component of a SATCOM antenna assembly.
  • STC — supplemental type certificate: the FAA approval covering a non-standard install, like a retrofitted terminal.
  • AFMS — aircraft flight manual supplement: documents any STC-installed system's limitations and placards.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the Wi-Fi work on the ground but drop right after takeoff?
Most cabin APs run on ground power/Wi-Fi through an FBO link before departure, which masks a dead onboard modem. If it drops right at rotation, the aircraft's own MCU never had a signal — check the link light before you blame the climb.
Can passengers fix this themselves?
Only steps 1, 4, and 5 above — AP reboot, SSID rejoin, and checking their own device settings. Anything touching the modem, breaker panel, or antenna bay is crew or maintenance territory.
Does bad weather actually kill SATCOM Wi-Fi?
Yes — heavy rain or wet-snow attenuation (rain fade) on Ka-band is a real, physics-level throughput hit, not a hardware fault. It clears when the aircraft exits the cell; no reset fixes it faster.
When to stop and call maintenance: a fault code that survives one breaker cycle, a modem that never syncs after AP and CB resets, or any sign of physical antenna/radome damage. Open a squawk against ATA Chapter 23 and let an avionics shop pull it — not the pilot, not the FA, not the passenger who's "pretty good with routers."

Keep it from happening again

  • Have connectivity firmware updated at scheduled maintenance, not as an emergency AOG item mid-trip.
  • Confirm the data plan renewal date before a long trip, especially over a fleet change of hands.
  • Brief passengers on the current SSID and password each leg — CMS resets sometimes regenerate it.
  • Log recurring squawks by tail number. "Drops every flight past 30W" is a beam-coverage or antenna-alignment issue an avionics shop can actually fix — re-diagnosing it fresh every flight never does.
